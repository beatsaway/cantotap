Negotiation tips — for my reference

Private checklist when discussing a project with a client.

Before quoting

  • Get their budget range — “Do you have a budget in mind for this?” so you don’t over- or under-price.
  • Clarify must-haves vs nice-to-haves — Scope the MVP first; extras = extra fee.
  • Who decides and who pays? — Decision-maker and budget holder; avoid long chains of approval.
  • Timeline — Rush = premium or reduced scope. Agree a realistic deadline.

When they say “too expensive”

Don’t drop price immediately. Ask: “What would need to change for this to work?” Then reduce scope (fewer features, phases) or timeline (fewer revisions), not just the number.
Offer 2 options: “Option A at full scope — $X. Option B with [fewer features] — $Y.” Let them choose.

What to get in writing

  • Scope: list of deliverables / features (even short).
  • Fee (total or hourly + cap) and currency (HKD).
  • Payment: deposit % and when; when the rest is due (e.g. on delivery).
  • Revision rounds included (e.g. 2 rounds); what counts as “new scope”.
  • Timeline: key dates or phases.

Email confirmation is enough for small projects; consider a short agreement for larger ones.

Red flags to watch

Vague scope — “We’ll add things as we go.” Push for a phase 1 scope; anything extra = change order / new quote.
Payment after “everything is done” — Always get a deposit (e.g. 30–50%) before starting. Milestone payments for longer projects.
“We have no budget but we’ll give you exposure” — Say no unless you intentionally do pro bono. Exposure doesn’t pay rent.
Endless revisions — Cap rounds in the scope (e.g. 2 rounds). Extra rounds = extra fee or fixed add-on.

During the project

  • Scope creep — New requests = “That’s outside the agreed scope. I can quote an add-on.”
  • Stick to the scope doc — If they keep adding, refer back: “This wasn’t in the original list; we can add it for $X.”
  • Don’t deliver full project before final payment — Hand over code/site after the agreed balance is received (or use escrow if needed).

Quick script

“For a project like this I usually work with a fixed scope and fee. I’ll send you a short scope and quote. Once you confirm and the deposit is paid, we’ll start. Any changes to scope we’ll agree in writing and adjust the fee or timeline.”
